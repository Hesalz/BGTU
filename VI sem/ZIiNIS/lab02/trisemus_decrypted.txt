How wonderful it all began! They took their seats on the chronobus, and the radiant lady accompanying them announced:

"Dear tourists! Our company, Retro-tour, is pleased to welcome you to the Moscow-2018 route. We guarantee you an unforgettable experience."

Petka fidgeted in his seat, anticipating bragging to his friends. A vacation on Mars or Venus—no one would be surprised. The Moon has long been a weekend destination. But time travel is a new and prestigious endeavor.

Standard retro tour packages offered "immersion into the everyday life of that era." Additional options, such as participation in the Battle of Poltava or the construction of St. Petersburg, were paid for separately.

Petka dreamed of going to some war, but his parents nipped his idea in the bud.

His mother wanted to visit 17th-century Versailles. It was very tempting to hang out among the courtiers of Louis XIV. My father dismissed that option, too.

"You don't know what you want! Oh, beautiful! And have you thought about the unsanitary conditions? They were simply filthy. That's why they invented perfume to mask the smell. And women hid boxes of special powder in their hair to repel fleas."

"Ugh!"

So, they decided to go to Moscow in 2018. It was inexpensive, the time was calm, and not entirely in the dark. After all, the World Wide Web was already operational.

Their chronobus materialized on the outskirts of Moscow. It looked like an ordinary tourist bus, the kind found in abundance in the capital.

Two weeks flew by in a flash. They enioyed shopping in supermarkets; it was so unlike zero-delivery! The lack of hovercars was unnerving; they had to sit in traffic iams with Muscovites, but they unanimously decided it was part of the local exotica. The roller coaster seemed like a museum exhibit, but it provided a top-notch adrenaline rush. Petka insisted on visiting McDonald's, even though his father had argued at length about self-inflicted poisoning.

The company looked after them like infants. In the morning, a red-and-white bus picked them up at the hotel door and delivered them in the evening.

And now they had been sitting in the lobby with their luggage for half a day, waiting to leave, fiddling with the smartphone the company had given them. The contact number wasn't answering. Their guardians had vanished into thin air. Meanwhile, the friendly smile of the receptionist on duty became strained, and then vanished altogether.

The internet of 2018 knew nothing about the travel agency of the future.
